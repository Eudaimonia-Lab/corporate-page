Philosophy

Transforming Conflict
into Emergence.

Harnessing Duality. Elevating Society.

Purpose

Conflict, elevated, becomes eudaimonia. The total grows.

Settled, a conflict costs someone. Elevated, it pays everyone.

Conflict rests on the premise that one side wins. Aufhebung breaks that premise and produces something neither side held. The total grows. This is what we call abundance: the operation that turns taking from one another into creating.

Seen from the side of each person, it describes a state in which eudaimonia (being well) and ikigai (a reason to get up in the morning) hold at the same time. Our company name is the name of that world.

Glossary

On our terms

Aufhebung is Hegel's concept for a movement that negates and preserves at the same time, lifting what it negates to a higher level. In Japanese it is rendered as 止揚 (shiyō). We use the German term as the name of a methodology for organizational work.

The three-step formula of thesis and antithesis to Synthese is not Hegel's own terminology. It originates with Fichte and was popularized by Heinrich Moritz Chalybäus. We use the formula as a device for explanation, and place the origin of the concept in Aufhebung.

We write Synthese, the German, rather than synthesis, because synthesis slides easily into meaning a mere compromise.

The concrete methodology for diagnosing the type of a conflict and designing a response is set out in Conflictology. For the framework we use to read a person's internal structure, see Human OS.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is Aufhebung (Synthese)?
Aufhebung is Hegel's concept for a movement that negates and preserves at the same time, lifting what it negates to a higher level. It neither erases a conflict nor lets one side win: it produces an answer that neither side held. Note that the three-step formula of thesis and antithesis to Synthese is not Hegel's own terminology; it originates with Fichte.
What is a Synthese Society?
A society that makes use of conflict rather than erasing it. It holds thesis and antithesis in opposition and waits for a higher-order answer (Synthese) to emerge from them. We write Synthese, the German, rather than synthesis, because synthesis slides easily into meaning a mere compromise.
